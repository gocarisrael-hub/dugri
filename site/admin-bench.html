What this bench is, and is not

  

    The artwork is the nine live סיישל card files, and the boxes start at the calibration the press
    is using today. The geometry you drag is exact — those fractions are the ones the generator
    reads.
  

  

    The fit is the generator's own, ported: _balanced_split,
    _hard_split, _candidates,
    _fit_card, _lead_for and
    _card_lead with its column-by-column
    _min_line_pitch — same constants, same search, same tie-breaks. Checked
    against the real thing on sixteen cards of a live order: identical line counts, and the sizes
    agreed to 0.03. The one piece that could not come across whole is the rasteriser —
    _ink_skyline reads inked pixels through PIL, this reads them through a
    canvas — and that difference is what the 0.03 is.
  

  

    The paper the words may reach is read off the artwork here, the way
    room_bottom reads it off the printed frame. Two dashed lines on the
    selected card show where it starts and ends; a wrapped line is paid for out of that.
  

  
The box always wins

  

    Every number here — a ceiling, a pinned size, the English word size — stops at the box it has to
    print inside. The type can grow past what you see whenever the words get shorter, because the
    width fit relaxes with the text; it can never grow past the
    height fit, because that one is the rectangle itself. So the sliders top
    out there, and the only way to a bigger number is a bigger box.
  

  

    The four ceilings and the English word size do not exist in the generator yet. Pick the numbers
    here, and they get wired in as word_max_he,
    word_max_en, title_max_en,
    title_max_he and word_en_size /
    word_alt_scale.
